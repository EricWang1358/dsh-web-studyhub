- **Single- and multiple-choice questions are harder to answer without knowing the material.** Wrong options are now written as the specific mistakes a learner of that material makes, and the review fails a question where a wrong option can be ruled out by a cue (the right answer is the longest, a word of the question is repeated only in it, "always/never" only in wrong options, "all of the above"). Questions with a repeated option, "all/none of the above", a clearly longest right answer, "always/never" in several wrong options or a question phrase echoed only by the right option are sent back for repair, and the same rule is given to personalised variants and to the rewrite for bad options. Not verified: tested with a fake model and hand-written examples, not measured how many of a real model's questions are sent back or whether learners find the options harder.
