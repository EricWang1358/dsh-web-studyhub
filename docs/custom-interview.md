# Customizing a big book in depth (the interview)

[中文](custom-interview.zh-CN.md)

A learner who uploads a big book (500 pages, say) does not need every part of it made. The main chat asks a few questions with options, one at a time, and only what the learner ticks is generated. The answers are saved as the course's **custom plan** (the `custom` record of the course) and drive the work.

## Where it starts

- **Customize this book in depth** on the row of a converted or imported document of 150 pages or more: in the file list of **Add source** and in the **Conversion history** (**PDF parsing**, and the Sources page once there is a record). It is offered once for a document: after it is pressed, that row does not offer it again. Shorter documents never show it. The 150 is one constant, `CUSTOM_BOOK_PAGES` in `ui/custom-entry.js`.
- **More › Whole book › Customize in depth…** on the review book, for every course, whatever the pages.

Both only fill the main chat's input box (the shared hand-off, `custom.start`); the learner presses send. Nothing is generated by pressing them. The message names the course, and the document key and page count when the row knows them.

## The interview (skill `study-custom`)

The chat reads the course and the read-only preview of the plan once (what the book holds, chapter by chapter, and a rough estimate of calls and tokens; no model call), then asks, with `ask_user_question`, in this order:

1. What you use the book for: preview, follow the course, final revision, open-book exam. This sets the defaults of every later question, and later questions offer the default first.
2. What to cover: the whole book, only the exam scope (paste or upload the syllabus), or some chapters (typed ranges such as 3–7 are accepted).
3. Whether to make the review book, which parts (explanations, term cards and confusables, answer templates and cheat sheet, formula pages and index), and how detailed.
4. Whether to make questions, which kinds, how many (the coverage strength), and whether to prepare variants of missed questions.
5. A plan: by exam date, by chapters per week, or none; minutes a day.
6. Confirm: a sample first (recommended), everything at once, or trim it further.

After each answer the chat reads the estimate again and says the change in one line (calls and tokens, never prices). Nothing is generated and nothing is saved before the confirm. Then it saves the plan with `course.save` and calls **`course.custom.apply`** once (see below), which starts what the plan asks for through the existing jobs and answers in plain words what it started, what waits and why, and what is only recorded. A choice no feature can carry out yet (the exam range, leaving a single part out of the review book) is saved in the plan, listed under `recorded`, and the chat says plainly that it was not done.

## Starting the work (`course.custom.apply`)

`course.custom.apply {course?, confirm?}` starts what a **saved** plan asks for, in this order, through jobs that already exist (it has none of its own): the review book (`generation.courseBook.build`, the picked chapters at the plan's detail level), the questions (`generate`, see below), the study plan (`course.save {plan}`). It answers `{status, course, started, waiting, recorded, done}`; every item is `{step, what}` in plain words for the chat to read out, and the items of a job carry its `jobId`:

- `started`: what was started now (the book's task, one task for each group of chapters' questions, the plan saved).
- `waiting`: what was not started and why: no model, a sample that waits for you, a chapter id that names nothing now, a refusal of the build, more than 12 groups of questions at once (say it again when these have finished), an exam date a plan by date needs.
- `recorded`: choices that are only kept in the plan (the exam range; the parts of the book while the build cannot leave one out).
- `done`: what is already so: a written book, questions that exist, a saved plan, the variants switch.

It is safe to say again: a task that is going is named, not started twice; a book that is written and questions that exist are not made again. With **sample first** the book writes its two-point sample and waits, and the questions start for the first group only, its first round; `confirm: true` (you said it was fine) builds the rest of the book and starts every group.

**Questions follow the plan.** They read only the pages of the chosen chapters (a chapter of the outline: the pages its points stand for; a chapter of the material: its pages; a chapter inside one text with no pages can only be read whole, and the answer says so), only the kinds you ticked (one mixed run, not one run for each kind) and at the plan's strength. A coverage run is planned for at most 500 questions, so the cap is per run: the chosen chapters are packed in reading order into groups that fit under it (a chapter that is over it by itself is cut at its own pages), one task for each group. A 1,500-question plan over three chapters is three or more tasks, not one task cut at 500. The preview's question count and calls come from the same grouping (`questions.runs` says how many tasks).

**A weekly plan.** `plan.by: 'weekly'` with `chaptersPerWeek` saves a 备考计划 that needs no exam date: the chapters of the review book are laid out week after week from the day it was saved, every study day of a week carries that week's points, and what was not learnt when its week is over moves into the current week. The review book's **Study plan** section and the home's **This week** card say *This week: chapters 3–4* and today's points. A plan by exam date needs the exam date first (apply says so and saves nothing without it) and is saved as a minimal plan; a course that already has a plan is never overwritten.

**Variants.** The plan's `variants` switch is for this course only: on, the wrong answers of this course get variants prepared automatically (the practice debrief, a follow-up, a too-easy or too-hard vote), without the general consent in Settings being switched on; off, they do not, whatever the general consent says; no plan (or a plan that makes no questions), the general consent decides as before. What you ask for by hand in the wrong book is not touched.

A course that already has a plan is not interviewed again from the start: the chat says the plan in one sentence and asks only what changed.

If the host does not allow multi-select, the multi-choice questions are asked as a short typed reply that lists the options.
