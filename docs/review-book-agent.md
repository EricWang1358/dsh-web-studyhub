# The review book and the main chat

[中文](review-book-agent.zh-CN.md)

You can ask the main DSH chat to write or rewrite your [review book](review-book.md) (复习全书): one point, a chapter or the whole book, in your own words ("rewrite 2.3 for a beginner, with an example") or by sending it the part to change. Asking in chat is the permission: the chat saves without asking again, and every write keeps the version before it, so **Back to the previous version** on the book page (or asking the chat to undo) takes it back. The one hard rule is that the book respects your materials: what it says comes from the materials, quotes are checked word for word, and anything beyond them is marked （资料以外）. Checked against the code on 2026-10-16.

## How the chat works on the book

The chat follows the `study-book` skill that StudyHub ships (`skills/study-book/SKILL.md` in the plugin). It works one knowledge point at a time:

1. It reads the table of contents (`book.toc`): each point's id, what is written, and how many of its pages were already read.
2. It reads one point (`book.read`): its files, its headings, the previous and the next point, and the material pages the point stands on.
3. It opens those pages (`source.pages`, at most 4 pages a call). The app records which pages were opened, so the chat (and you) can see how much of a point's material was really read (`book.coverage`).
4. It writes the point heading by heading (`book.write`). Every quote is a footnote naming its material; the app looks the words up in that material (else in the point's own pages) and takes out a footnote whose words are not there, telling the chat which one. A quote taken out twice for the same point comes with the advice to delete the sentence. A footnote mark and its footnote go together: the file always ends with one list `[^1]`…`[^n]`, a footnote left exactly as the file has it is kept as written when it cannot be found again (one the write adds or whose words it changes is always checked), and an edit that takes out a footnote whose marks are still in the text is refused.
5. It reads the result, checks the coverage and goes on to the next point. At the end it tells you, in Chinese, which points changed, which quotes were taken out, what was marked as beyond the materials and which pages were not read.

What the chat writes into a point's generated text becomes yours: rebuilding the explanations later no longer replaces it, the new generated text only waits as a suggestion (**Take it** / **Ignore**), as when you edit the page yourself. Your own notes and the Q&A of a point are changed only when you name them or ask for the whole book to be rewritten.

## Reorganising the book

You can also ask the chat to tidy up the book's structure: rename a chapter or a point ("call 2.3 'Processes and threads'"), move a point to another chapter or next to another point, delete a point or a chapter you do not need, and inside one point insert, rename, move or delete a section. None of this reorganises the outline or sends the book's text: the chat names chapters, points and headings by their ids. A new section goes where it belongs instead of the end of the file. Inserting or renaming a section refuses a title the point's file already has (spaces, letter case and the full-width or half-width form of a punctuation mark do not make a title different: 十一、性能测试 and 十一，性能测试 are the same title; the numbering does: 十二、性能测试 is another); a write of the whole point, of one section or a replacement that leaves a title twice is reported to the chat, never silent. A deleted point or section leaves no empty heading and no placeholder text, and what you wrote in a deleted point is never thrown away: it waits in 未归位 under the point's title. Deleting a chapter puts its points in its place (or, if you ask, removes them too); a chapter left with no point stays, empty, until it is deleted too. A move that would push a heading deeper than level 6 (or above level 1) is refused. Every change keeps the version before it: ask the chat to undo it (it uses `course.book.revert`), the latest first, until the outline is made again (**Reorganise**, or **Update the book** when it makes a new outline because materials or sample papers changed) and for the last 10 structure changes (fewer when the points deleted were large). An undo never throws away what you changed since: a file you edited after the change (or took back with **Back to the previous version**) is left as it is, and the undo says which. **Back to the previous version** itself never throws a text away either: the chat can bring back what it replaced (`redo`). When the outline is made again, the book is laid out by the new outline: the titles you gave points stay with them, a chapter's title only when the new outline names the chapter as before, and moves and deletions are not kept.

## Sending it from the book page

The book page can fill the chat's input box for you (it never sends; you read the message and press send). The message opens with your own sentence (what you want of which point, the point's title as a name) and right under it your request in a fence, so the first thing in the box is what you asked. Below a blank line, under the head **StudyHub notes (for the assistant)**, one labelled line each says what the chat needs: load the `study-book` skill and read the point with `book.read`, the course and the point, the point's files with the version of its generated file, how many of its material pages were read, the rules (verified footnotes, the beyond-the-materials tag in the book's language, one section at a time), what to report (each change keeps the previous version, `course.book.revert` takes it back), and one line saying what a fence holds; the selected words follow in a data fence, and the language sentence ends it. It carries no book text: the chat reads the point itself.

- **Ask AI** at a point's title (see [Ask AI while reading](review-book.md#ask-ai-while-reading)): one box with five presets (**Explain in more detail**, **Add an example**, **Polish into plain language**, **Add transitions**, **Which smaller points are missing, and should they be added?**) and two destinations; **Send to the main chat** is the one that goes here. The box may be empty (the message then asks the chat to make the point clearer and easier to read). Escape closes it and the focus goes back to **Ask AI**. It does not need a model of the app.
- Selected words: the toolbar's **Send to the main chat** sends them at once (in a data fence, without the footnote digits) with the same point, and asks the chat to change only what concerns them; **Write a paragraph…** opens the same box with the words on top, and its **Send to the main chat** adds what you typed.
- **More › Whole book › Send to the main chat** in the book bar sends at once (no form: add a request in the chat's input box if you like); the message has no point, asks the chat which points are worth changing first and to write only after you choose, and tells it to start with `book.toc` and go one point at a time.

What happened is said by a receipt at the bottom of the page, a small stamped slip: **In the main chat's box** (it leaves after 5 seconds; the app has already switched to the chat), **Your typed text is kept** when you had typed something there (the message is copied instead; **Go to the main chat** where the host can show it), **Copied to the clipboard** when the host has no chat box (the app is opened on its own; **See it** opens the message). When the browser refuses the clipboard, the whole message opens in a card with **Copy all** and a select-all fallback; only closing it without copying leaves the slip **Copy it by hand**. You can also just tell the main chat how you want the book changed, without any button.

## Long work

The chat's calls are small on purpose: the host cuts long tool answers, and in Auto permission mode it reviews every call with all the earlier ones, so a conversation that writes whole files would overflow. When a conversation gets long, the chat finishes the point it is on, says where it stopped and suggests a new conversation; in Auto mode one conversation per chapter works best. Write "go on from 3.2" in the new conversation.

### If the permission review itself fails

The permission review ("auto-review") is part of DSH, not of StudyHub: StudyHub does not run it, and it declares nothing that switches it on or off. If the host's review service is down or rejects its own request (the chat sees `INVALID_REQUEST` / 400, on reads such as `book.toc` as well as on writes), nothing in the book has been changed by that call. Wait a few minutes and ask again, start a new conversation (a long one makes the review's request larger), or approve the call by hand in a permission mode that asks you. The chat is told to stop and say so rather than retry in a loop or turn to a heavier call such as `course.book.save`. An outage like this is for the DSH project to fix; StudyHub cannot repair it.

## Making the skill available

When the plugin starts in a DSH that has the skill registry, it registers `study-book` itself, and the chat's skill list shows it in every session that has the skill tool (the study preset has it). If your chat does not show `study-book`, copy the folder `skills/study-book` of the installed plugin to `~/.dsh/skills/study-book` (so that the file is `~/.dsh/skills/study-book/SKILL.md`); DSH reads skills from there too. The plugin itself never writes outside its own folder and the library.

## For developers

- `book.toc {course?, offset?, limit?}` (at most 40 rows a call), `book.read {course?, hid, file?: 'gen' | 'mine' | 'qa', offset?}` (4 500 characters of text together, `more: {file, offset}` goes on), `book.coverage {course?, hid}`: lib/book-agent-read.js, read only; `book.toc` first brings the book up to date as `course.book.open` does (it creates it, maps it to a rebuilt outline). Each answer stays under the host's 8 192-character cut of a tool result. For a book written by the quality workflow ([course outline](course-outline.md#the-review-book-explanation-layer)), `book.toc` also says each point's `concepts: 'N/M'` (the must items of its checklist covered) with `short: true` for a point kept short, and the book's three-level `coverage`; `book.coverage` adds `concepts {covered, total, status, missing?}` with the names still missing. A file the answer cut also carries `truncated: true` and `nextOffset`; `offset` only works together with `file` (without it the call is refused with `book-offset-needs-file`, never silently read from the start).
- `source.pages {sourceId | documentId, from?, to?, offset?, hid?, course?}`: lib/book-pages.js, at most 4 pages and 6 000 characters (`next: {from, offset?}`, `end: true`); the library's own lists (exam point lists, the outline, the book) are refused. What it showed is recorded by lib/book-reading.js in `book-history/reading.json` under the library folder (ranges of characters per source, the sources a point's work opened, the quotes a point's writes dropped), written through a temporary file and a rename; it is not in the library and not in a portable backup.
- `book.write {course?, hid, file?, markdown | section: {heading, markdown} | edits: [{find, replace}], version?}`: lib/book-agent-write.js with the footnote rules of lib/book-footnotes.js and the quote locator lib/book-quote.js (shared with the review book build). At most 20 000 characters a call and 20 edits. It runs in the book's writer queue (lib/course-book-writer.js) and one store transaction through `course.book.save`'s rule (lib/course-book-edit.js), so a save from the book page and a write from the chat are served one after the other. Footnotes are written back in the book's form, `[^n]: [「words」](studyhub://source/<id>) · title`, numbered in order as one list under **出处** (checked before saving; a file that holds several lists of `[^1]`…`[^n]` is repaired to one, each mark keeping the footnote of the list after it; a footnote whose text is unchanged from the file and is not found again is kept as written and named in the warnings, in edits, a section and a whole-file write that copies it unchanged; a whole-file write that leaves out the footnote and its marks drops it, and one that keeps a mark but leaves the footnote out keeps it too (the mark cites the file's footnote of that number, with a warning); a mark of a new text with no footnote of its own cites the file's footnote of that number, with a warning; a definition may be typed with `：`). A quote is read to its own closing quotation mark, and a footnote mark is never part of its words (the comparison key of lib/quote-match.js and lib/quote-locate.js leaves `[^n]` out); headings are put below the point's own heading; the 练 5 道 line stays last; Q&A, answer-card and AI blocks are kept as they are. A stale `version` on a whole-file write saves the chat's text and keeps the other as a suggestion; a section or edits are applied to the file as it is now. Answer: `{status, hid, file, name, version, dropped: [{n, quote, reason, again?}], outsideCount, warnings, advice?, note?}`; warnings name another language than the book's, pages not opened yet, and blocks no longer in the file. Refusals: book-write-mode, book-write-too-long, book-edit-find, book-footnote-in-use (edits take out a footnote still marked: the marks and their count are named), book-footnote-check, book-section-missing, book-file-invalid, book-point-missing, book-point-not-leaf, course-book-missing.
- `book.write` with `op: 'insert' | 'delete' | 'rename' | 'move'` (#459, lib/book-section-ops.js): the sections of one point's file as a tree, named by heading id (book.read lists them), never by title. insert `{section: {heading, markdown}, after | before | within: id, or position: 'start' | 'end'}` (a heading of the same title is refused with book-section-exists; `sameTitle` ignores all whitespace, letter case and the full-width / half-width form of a punctuation mark, with 、 a comma and 。 a full stop, and keeps words, digits and numbering; `section.id` of the answer is the new heading's id); delete `{heading, children?: 'promote' | 'delete' | 'toUnplaced'}` (promote, the default, lifts the sub-sections one level; toUnplaced also writes 未归位, the section's footnotes renamed `[^<hid>x<n>-<key>]` with their definitions, refused with course-book-unplaced-full when 未归位 would pass `FILE_TEXT_MAX`; that file keeps its own previous version); rename `{heading, title}`; move `{heading, after | before | within | position}` (the levels follow the new place; never inside itself, never past level 6: book-section-move-invalid). The same 20 000-character rule, footnote checks and `version` rule as a section write. Refusals: book-write-op (it names the allowed ops and places), book-write-mode, book-section-missing, book-section-exists, book-section-move-invalid, course-book-unplaced-full. Rename, move and delete never check or renumber the file's footnotes (they keep the file's own, a learner's note too, under its own 出处 label); insert checks its section's like a section write. A section write that names a title the file does not have still adds it at the end, with a warning that points at op insert; any write that leaves one title twice in the file warns (`the file now has the heading "…" twice`). The book's own chapters and points: `course.book.rename / move / delete` ([review book, for developers](review-book.md#for-developers)), compare-and-set on `structureVersion`, which book.toc says.
- 「发给主会话」: the message is the registry's `book.edit` (ui/agent-prompts/index.js, skill `study-book`); `bookEditPrompt({course, hid?, title?, files, version, coverage, selection?, instruction, scope: 'point' | 'selection' | 'book'}, language)` in ui/agent-prompts/book.js returns its parts and ui/agent-prompts/message.js lays them out (pure; both languages; no book text; the title a name span, the selected words in a data fence, the learner's request in a request fence). The box is ui/book/BookAsk.jsx (`AskForm`), the selection toolbar ui/book/BookSelectBar.jsx, both wired in ui/book/BookAskLayer.jsx; the whole book is 更多's item (ui/book/more-actions.js); the way out is `useSendToChat(course)` in ui/book/send-to-chat.js (reads `book.coverage` for the point, then the shared unit ui/hand-off/: the host's chat box, else the clipboard, else the full-text card). Tests: tests/book-send-to-chat.test.mjs, tests/book-send-to-chat-browser.test.mjs.
- The skill is lib/study-skills.js: `skills/<name>/SKILL.md` (YAML frontmatter `name`, `description`, `whenToUse`) registered with `ctx.skills.register` when the host injects `skills`. The tool contract is area `book` of `library.context`.
